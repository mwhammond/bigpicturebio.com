## The core insight

The advisory board is, underneath the detail, telling us one thing:

> **Pharma decision-makers don't buy capabilities. They buy de-risked decisions on the assets they already care about — framed in the language of this quarter's discourse and their own KPIs.**

Everything in the feature list (combination accuracy, resistance maps, multiscale interpretability, >85% prospective) is *how we're credible*. It is not *what they're buying*. Nobody wakes up wanting a "world model"; they wake up needing to know whether to run the trial, what to combine their backbone with, and whether the field is about to make their asset irrelevant.

So the compression rule is: **lead with the decision, prove with the engine.**

One engine → three decisions:

| | Decision they're making | Their words for it | Primary buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Design** | *What combination makes my asset win — and keep winning?* | "augment the backbone, one step ahead"; "increase asset value long-term"; "franchise still relevant" | Global project leader · Franchise lead · Discovery |
| **2. De-risk** | *Will this trial actually read out positive?* | "increase chance of success (20% is expected)"; "reduce time / patients / cost"; "right stats for complex sequences" | Clinical development plan owner · Global project leader |
| **3. Decide** | *Where is the field going, and is this asset worth the bet?* | "was that acquisition a good buy?"; "what becomes new SoC?"; "BD feels like a coin toss until clinical" | Strategy · Portfolio · BD / Corporate Development · Investors |

**Design. De-risk. Decide.** One world model, pointed at the three decisions that move a pipeline.

## The three flagship offerings

Each is written as: the promise → the job it does → what we deliver → why us → the KPI it moves → a live anchor from current discourse.

### 1 — Combination Architect
**"Turn your backbone into the durable standard of care."**

- **The job.** You own a promising asset or backbone class. Alone it's a good drug; the question is what combination, sequence, dose schedule and patient setting turn it into the therapy that *stays* first-line — and what the OS/PFS actually is before you commit a trial.
- **What we deliver.** A generative search across tens of thousands of candidate combinations, co-optimised not just for efficacy but for tox, delivery and clinical simplicity — narrowed to a short list of designed regimens, each with a predicted endpoint (OS/PFS/adherence) and a **resistance map** of known *and* generated escape routes so it holds up as the disease evolves.
- **Why us.** Additive *and* synergistic effects in dynamic diseases are exactly where existing tools are weakest and the therapeutic upside is largest. We predict combinations even where none of the drugs have been tested together, and design *around* resistance even where a target or MoA has never been in the clinic.
- **KPI it moves.** Long-term asset value · franchise durability · one step ahead of the backbone.
- **Live anchor.** The KRAS backbone question — everyone's augmenting the inhibitor; who's modelling what to put *around* it and in what order? IO sequencing (checkpoints in sequence vs. combination). TOPO + microtubule dual-payload ADC interactions.

### 2 — Trial Compass
**"Design the trial that reads out positive — on fewer patients, in less time."**

- **The job.** Before you commit a phase, you need the odds moved off the ~20% base rate, and you need to defend the design choices: line of therapy, population, dose step-up, stratification, out-patient setting, placebo/site risk.
- **What we deliver.** A predicted endpoint outcome for the trial as designed, a stress-test of the design levers that raise probability of success while cutting patient numbers / time / cost, and — crucially — **the single in-vitro / in-vivo de-risking experiment that most changes the odds**, so R&D spend goes to the one result that matters. Reasoning-based, with calibrated confidence and statistics built for complex interactions and sequences (not the wrong test applied to a synergy).
- **Why us.** Multiscale and interpretable all the way down: from receptor engagement in a single cell population, across metastases and patient sub-groups, up to trial-site placebo risk. That's what lets us reduce stratification (we know *which* patients and why), de-risk dose step-up / CRS ahead of time, and reason a 2nd-line signal into a 1st-line win.
- **KPI it moves.** Probability of success · patient numbers · time-to-outcome · cost · out-patient feasibility.
- **Live anchor.** BiTE dose step-up and CRS risk. "Show efficacy in 2nd line, then win 1st line." Reducing stratification without losing the signal.

### 3 — Landscape Engine
**"Mechanistic competitive intelligence — and deal diligence that isn't a coin toss."**

- **The job.** Strategy and BD are advising on where the field goes and which assets to back. Today that's headline-reading and analyst decks. They need the *mechanism*, not the press release: what becomes standard of care, what a competitor's combination does to your franchise, and whether an in-licensing target actually works — in which setting, in which population.
- **What we deliver.** Mechanistic answers to the questions already on the strategy/BD whiteboard, tied to the catalysts and conference debates they're tracking: what a readout in the next 3–6 months implies, what new trials will have to compare against, what a given acquisition is really worth. For BD/diligence specifically, a **mechanistic verdict** on an asset — "this will work, but only combined with X / in this indication / this population" — that turns "coin toss until clinical" into something defensible.
- **Why us.** We work from a small number of key facts, not endless data-trawling, so we can turn around a named, current question fast — and every answer carries a calibrated confidence and an interpretable chain of reasoning a committee can interrogate.
- **KPI it moves.** Competitive-landscape confidence · catalyst planning · diligence quality · defended revenue.
- **Live anchor.** "Was the Myricx Bio buy a good one?" "Will Harmoni-6 (Chinese asset) translate to a Western squamous-lung population, and with what modifier?" Post-GLP-1 world. Teccreso off-patent 2032 — what replaces it. Immunology catching up with oncology — what transfers and what doesn't.

## How we deliver it — engagement models (not offerings)

These are *how the three offerings get sold and scoped*, and matter for the BD page's "work with us" section:

- **Partnered discovery / advisory** — scoped answer to a specific asset or landscape question (fastest wedge; matches "identify assets, risks, combinations" in the original service list).
- **Co-development** — partner on our own in-house programmes across oncology, immunology and neuro; or joint programmes on a partner's asset with shared upside.
- **Independent certification** — third-party mechanistic validation of a program for biotechs and their investors bringing an asset toward the clinic. This is a distinct wedge: it sells to the *capital* side, not just pharma R&D, and it's low-friction to start. (From the original doc: *"work as certifier for biotechs and investors… this will work / this will not work by itself but needs to partner with X / this indication is much better."*)

Recommendation: **lead the website and deck with Design / De-risk / Decide; present the three engagement models as the "ways to work with us" footer.** Don't make certification a fourth pillar on equal footing — it dilutes the story — but do keep it visible because it's the easiest first cheque and it reaches investors.

## Anchoring to live discourse (the advisory board's strongest signal)

The board was emphatic: **get into the conversation that's already happening** — bank-analyst topics, press, conference discourse (ESMO next). Practical implication for the site and deck:

- Every offering should carry **1–2 named, current examples** (as above). This is what makes a "world model" concrete and signals we're *in* the debate, not observing it.
- Keep a short, rotating "**what we're modelling now**" strip — the live questions (KRAS backbone, TOPO in PDAC, CD47 rebound in breast, post-GLP-1, oral SERDs, Harmoni-6 translation). This doubles as SEO and as proof of currency for anyone who lands on the page mid-conference.
- Bias examples toward **active-portfolio, backbone-augmentation** framing. The board flagged that getting anyone to look at *shelved* assets is hard — so don't lead with "revive dead drugs," lead with "make your best asset unbeatable."

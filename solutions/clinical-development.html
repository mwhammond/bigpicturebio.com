The problem

Most of the risk is settled years before the read-out — at design.

By the time a trial reports, the choices that decided it — line, population, dose, endpoint — were locked in at design, when phase-to-phase success still sits near 20%. Four in five Phase III combinations fail, and the levers that could have changed that closed early.

01

Thin signal, big commitment. You green-light a Phase on early-phase data that rarely captures how a combination behaves at scale.

02

Failure modes set at design. Wrong line, population or dose step-up — and no amount of execution recovers it.

03

The wrong test on a synergy. Standard statistics miss the interactions and sequences that combinations live or die by.

Our reasoning model reads those choices the way the biology will, so you commit to the design that wins rather than the one you hope will — and every prediction is explainable, traceable from receptor engagement through to the endpoint. This matters most for novel immunotherapy combinations: foundation models and traditional ML can interpolate from known regimens, but cannot reliably extrapolate to a synergy that has never been observed. We reason from mechanism, cell state and resistance instead.
